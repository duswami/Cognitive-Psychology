# Cognitive Psychology

Cognitive psychology studies internal mental processes: how we perceive, attend, remember, think, solve problems, and make decisions. It treats the mind as an information processor.

## Core Areas
1. **Attention**: Filtering sensory input to prioritize relevant information. Selective attention (cocktail party effect), divided attention, sustained attention.
2. **Memory**: Encoding, storage, retrieval. Includes sensory, short-term/working, and long-term memory. Types: episodic, semantic, procedural.
3. **Perception**: Interpreting sensory data into meaningful experience. Bottom-up (data-driven) and top-down (knowledge-driven) processing. Gestalt principles (proximity, similarity, closure, continuity, figure-ground).
4. **Language**: Acquisition, comprehension, production.
5. **Problem-Solving & Reasoning**: Strategies, heuristics, algorithms.
6. **Decision-Making**: Weighing options under uncertainty; heuristics and biases.

## Key Concepts
- **Schemas**: Mental frameworks that organize knowledge and guide interpretation. Assimilation (fit new info into existing schema) vs. accommodation (change schema).
- **Working Memory**: Limited capacity system for holding and manipulating information (Baddeley model: central executive, phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, episodic buffer).
- **Long-Term Memory**: Unlimited capacity; explicit (declarative) vs. implicit (non-declarative).
- **Cognitive Biases**: Systematic deviations from rationality (e.g., confirmation bias, availability heuristic).
- **Executive Functions**: Planning, inhibition, cognitive flexibility, working memory.

## Key Figures
- Ulric Neisser: "Cognitive Psychology" (1967), father of the field
- George Miller: "The Magical Number Seven, Plus or Minus Two" (working memory capacity)
- Alan Baddeley: Working memory model
- Endel Tulving: Episodic vs. semantic memory
- Daniel Kahneman & Amos Tversky: Heuristics and biases, prospect theory
- Jean Piaget: Cognitive development, schemas
- Noam Chomsky: Language acquisition (critique of pure behaviorism)

## The Cognitive Revolution
Shift from behaviorism in the 1950s-60s, influenced by linguistics, computer science, and information theory. The mind as a computer analogy became central.
